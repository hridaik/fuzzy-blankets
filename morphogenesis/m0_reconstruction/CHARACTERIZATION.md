## E2 — Process-noise sensitivity (HIDDEN-tier reproduction metric)

Fixed initial `v0` (seed 0), 10 noise seeds (reduced from 20), process noise
`std = 1/exp(8) ≈ 3.35e-4` (= `sqrt(1/G(1).V)`, `G(1).V=exp(16)` —
MODEL_SPEC.md §10; noise injected as i.i.d. Gaussian on the process's
position/secretion outputs each D-step, a disclosed simplification of
`spm_ADEM`'s temporally-correlated generalized noise — DISCREPANCIES.md §9).
Metric: Hungarian-matched mean per-cell distance between final cell positions
and template positions (`hidden`-tier reproduction-quality metric, **not** a
morphology/identity claim per Task E's instruction).

- Mean Hungarian distance: **0.4261**, std **0.0132**, range [0.4047, 0.4413]
  across the 10 seeds.
- **Finding**: at the source-code's own noise precision, final-configuration
  spread across seeds is small relative to the mean distance itself
  (CV ≈ 3%) — i.e. **the published noise level, as coded (`G(1).V=exp(16)`),
  implies a near-deterministic outcome given a fixed initial condition**, in
  this reduced solver. This is consistent with `G(1).V=exp(16)` being an
  intentionally near-noiseless process precision (MODEL_SPEC.md §4).

## E3 — Initial-condition sensitivity (HIDDEN-tier)

Two batches, 10 draws each (reduced from 50): (a) "paper-matched" —
`v0 ~ N(0, exp(2))` per `σ = 1/√exp(-2) = e ≈ 2.718`, directly derived from
the 2015 paper's stated log-precision −2 (confirmed primary source,
DISCREPANCIES.md §4); (b) "hardcoded-matched" — `v0 ~ N(0, 0.125²)`, matching
the canonical script's own `randn(n,n)/8`.

| Batch | mean Hungarian dist. | std | mean pairwise dist. between final configs | 2-cluster split (of 10) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| paper-matched (σ=2.718) | 0.4764 | 0.1301 | 5.520 | 4 / 6 |
| hardcoded-matched (σ=0.125) | 0.2858 | 0.0864 | 5.611 | 6 / 4 |

**Finding**: the wider (paper-matched) prior draw produces both a larger mean
distance to template *and* larger variance across draws than the narrower
(hardcoded) draw — directionally sensible (broader initial uncertainty →
more room for different final configurations) but the **mean pairwise
distance between final configurations is nearly identical between the two
batches (5.52 vs 5.61)**, i.e. in this reduced solver the two prior widths do
not produce qualitatively different diversity of outcomes at `n=10` draws.
2-cluster splits (roughly even, 4/6 and 6/4) show no obvious bimodality at
this sample size; **NOT DONE**: a rigorous cluster-count justification (e.g.
silhouette score, gap statistic) was not performed — the "simple descriptive
clustering" requested was implemented as a 2-means split only.

## E4 — Long horizon (512 bins)

Stationarity criterion: max per-bin displacement (positions) AND secretion
change, L2 norm over all cells, below threshold for 32 consecutive bins.

| Config | runtime (512 bins) | final Hungarian dist. | stationary from bin (thr=1e-3) | stationary from bin (thr=1e-4) | drifting at bin 512? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `vanilla8` | 5.84 s | 0.1774 | **never** | **never** | **yes** |
| `high_identity_k4` | 5.77 s | 0.1758 | bin 389 | **never** | no (at thr=1e-3) |

**Findings** (negative results, first-class per ground rules):
- `vanilla8` **never reaches stationarity** by either threshold within 512
  bins in this reduced solver — it is still drifting at bin 512.
- `high_identity_k4` (the one available Pio-Lopez perturbation, k=4 cells
  given a strong initial identity bias) reaches the looser stationarity
  threshold (1e-3) at bin 389, but not the tighter one (1e-4) — i.e. the
  perturbed config settles into small residual drift, sensitive to the
  chosen threshold.
- Both configs' final Hungarian distance to template (0.177–0.175) is
  **worse (larger)** than several of the noisier/shorter E2/E3 runs (32-bin
  horizon) reported above (0.286–0.476 there were 32-bin runs; direct
  comparison across horizons is not apples-to-apples since 32-bin runs also
  never reach convergence — flagged, not resolved, in OPEN_QUESTIONS.md).
- **Caveat, disclosed prominently**: this reduced solver (fixed learning
  rates, explicit-Euler, FD-approximated action gradient — DISCREPANCIES.md
  §9) may simply be slower to converge or have a different fixed point than
  `spm_ADEM`'s local-linearization scheme. **Non-convergence by 512 bins is a
  fact about this Python reconstruction, not necessarily about the published
  model.**

## E5 — Timescales (HIDDEN tier, unperturbed `vanilla8`, 512 bins)

- Peak combined per-bin change (position + secretion) occurs at **bin 12**.
- E-folding time after peak (bins until combined change drops to 1/e of its
  peak-bin value): **35 bins**.
- Per-cell bin-index at which that cell's max softmax expectation first
  exceeds 0.9: **all 8 cells: never (null), within 512 bins.**
  **Negative finding, first-class**: in this reduced solver, no cell's
  identity belief becomes strongly concentrated (>0.9) within the tested
  horizon, even though position/secretion dynamics show an early peak (bin
  12) and subsequent e-folding decay (35 bins). This suggests the
  softmax-belief dynamics and the action (position/secretion) dynamics
  operate on different, and in this reconstruction possibly decoupled,
  timescales — or that the fixed learning-rate/prior-precision combination
  used here (`LR_V = 1/exp(3)`, prior pull `exp(-2)·v`) keeps beliefs
  permanently soft. **NOT DONE**: distinguishing "genuinely soft convergence"
  from "solver artifact" would require the reference implementation, which is
  unavailable (DISCREPANCIES.md §8).

## E6 — Scaling

32-bin runs, `n_inner=8` D-steps/bin (solver.py default), single machine.

| Config | n cells | assembly success | runtime (32 bins) | runtime/bin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `n8_real_template` (published L=2) | 8 | yes | 0.363 s | 0.0113 s |
| `L4_larger_template` (published L=4, dead code in source) | **16** | yes | 0.833 s | 0.0260 s |
| `synthetic_n24` (constructed, see `code/synthetic_template.py`) | 24 | yes | 1.390 s | 0.0434 s |
| `synthetic_n32` (constructed) | 32 | yes | 2.024 s | 0.0632 s |

**Construction rule for synthetic templates** (declared, per Task E6):
cells placed on a `⌈√(2n)⌉`-side integer lattice, shuffled positions (seed
`12345+n`), cell-type code `((row+col) mod 4) + 1` — a deterministic
checkerboard cycle over the same 4-code alphabet the published templates use.
This is **not** derived from any paper and carries no biological claim.

**Finding**: runtime per bin scales roughly as expected for the FD-based
action-gradient computation (`O(n)` finite-difference perturbations, each an
`O(n²)` vectorized field evaluation ⇒ ~`O(n³)`): going from n=8→32 (4×) gives
a ≈5.6× per-bin runtime increase, roughly consistent with cubic scaling
(`4³=64`× would be the naive cubic prediction if constants were identical
across configs; the observed sub-cubic growth reflects numpy vectorization
overhead being relatively larger at small n). All four configurations
**assembled without NaN/Inf** ("assembly success" = no NaN/Inf in the position
trace) within the 32-bin horizon tested. **ESTABLISHED**, no reduction needed.

## Overall E-series caveat

All of the above characterizes **this session's reduced Python
reconstruction of the D-step dynamics**, run with fixed (not tuned, not
validated) learning-rate heuristics (`code/solver.py`: `LR_V = 1/exp(3)`,
`LR_A = 1/(exp(3)·exp(2))`). No sensitivity analysis of these heuristics'
effect on the E4/E5 convergence/timescale findings was performed in this pass
— flagged in OPEN_QUESTIONS.md as a priority follow-up before treating any
E4/E5 number as more than illustrative.
