## Findings
* **The memory still outlives every cell**: after all 24 cells are replaced the body is structurally broken and every cell (all new) is in state a — as in v3. The shape cannot be recovered, because the shape is decided by a mis-filled place set that the structure module cannot repair (0/4).
* **Replacement is slightly worse than v3 for structure (14/24 vs 17/24) and the defective bodies carry a wrong tail angle**, which shows that in v4 a place error propagates into the shape variable; the newcomer adopts the state at once (2 tu) and the body shape then follows the *state* (all intact bodies are at exactly +17.1°). The state is restored in every case, even when the body is not.
* **Cut fragments keep their state: 16/16 fragments** (x-cut 8/8, y-cut 8/8; ρ > ½ at 1,500 tu in every one, lowest 0.71), and the **tail fragment keeps the angle sign** (+19.5…+20.4°); the head fragment, lacking a tail, carries state a but no measurable tail angle.
* The only state change seen in V6 outside fusion is the **noisy y-cut fragment A drifting to ρ 0.71** (≥ ½; a state flip in 1,500 tu is predicted rare for ≥ 11 cells at σ_h 0.4) — not a switch.
* **Fusion has no systematic winner** (a+b noisy: consensus in 6/12 runs, 5 to a and 1 to b; 6 mixed); the dial adds no consensus pressure beyond v3 (fused bodies end fragmented as in v3).
