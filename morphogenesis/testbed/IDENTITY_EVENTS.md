# IDENTITY_EVENTS.md — Part I: identity-event support (I1) and behaviour checks (I2)

Code: `code/engine.py` (alive mask, N_cells ≠ n_slots, control inputs), `code/world.py` (events, ids, logs), `code/i2.py` (checks); data `data/i2a_replace.json`, `i2b_serial.json`, `i2b_serial_interval400.json`, `i2c_extrude.json`, `i2d_cut.json`, `i2e_fuse.json`; viewer exemplars `viz/output/testbed/*/v1_i2*.html`. All runs: adult body at the S1 operating point (log pi_s 2, log pi_prior −8, k_mu 1.4, k_a 0.4), deterministic unless noted. Labels: ESTABLISHED / PROVISIONAL / NOT DONE.

## I1 support — ESTABLISHED
* **N_cells decoupled from n_slots:** `make_engine(tmpl, P, N)`; logits are (N, n_slots); `engine.n` = cell positions (alive or dead), `engine.S` = slots. Fusion builds a 48-cell engine over the same 24 slots.
* **Alive mask** (control input `alive`): dead cells are frozen, secrete nothing into the field, and receive no noise; **permanent unique `cell_id`** (counter `next_id`, never reused, dead array positions get id −1 until reused by a new id).
* **Events (all logged in `World.events` with time, index, cell ids, parameters):** REMOVAL `remove(i)`; INSERTION `insert(pos)` (naive cell: logits N(0, 1/8²), secretion reset to 0, new id); REPLACEMENT `replace(i)` (= removal + insertion at the same position, new id, logs the replaced id); EXTRUSION `extrude(i, vec)` (tweezers, instantaneous displacement); CUT `cut(mask, vec)` (cells on one side moved apart as a fragment); FUSION `fuse(w1, w2, offset, angle)` (two bodies in one arena, each cell keeps logits over the 24 slots, ids offset).
* **Hidden exports gain the events:** `World.frames` carry per-frame `alive` and `cell_id`; `Experiment.hidden()` returns the event list, the hidden arena pose, the dose accounting and the private actuator mapping (never in the observable tier).
* Tests: `tests/test_engine.py` (6 pass) — faithfulness, CRN and exports are unchanged by the engine extensions (they default to the previous behaviour).

## I2 behaviour checks (one adult L body, seeded start; taxonomy adapted from the v2 protocol: SHAPE class L / R / DEFECT (fragmentation, extrusion, incomplete slots) / OTHER / fragment; SLOT COMPLETENESS = slots filled exactly once / duplicated / vacant by argmax belief; ROLES = argmax slot per cell; EVENTS = logged)

**(a) Single replacement, 24 cells (one run each, 400 time units).** The naive cell re-specifies into the vacated slot (belief > 0.5) in **9/24** runs (time to belief 0.5: 5, 5, 5, 5, 10, 15, 45, 140, 285 time units; belief 0.9 reached in 5 of them, within 20–30 units); in the other 15 the new cell does not take the vacated slot. Final body class after 400 units: **L (complete) in 9/24, DEFECT in 15/24**. By type of the replaced cell: type 1 (head) L 4 / DEFECT 4; type 2 (trunk) L 1 / DEFECT 3; type 3 (limb) L 1 / DEFECT 3; type 4 (tail) L 3 / DEFECT 5. Replacing a trunk or limb cell is the most disruptive (the neighbouring cells reassign slots: up to 18 vacant slots). So the body does not reliably repair a single replacement. ESTABLISHED (one body, deterministic; the 24 runs are the 24 cells).

**(b) Serial replacement until 100 % of the cells are new (Ship of Theseus).** One replacement every 100 time units in random order, then 400 units of settling, 4 seeds: **DEFECT in 4/4 (0 slots filled exactly once; 20–23 vacant, 3–7 components)**; all 24 ids new and 24 unique ids in each run. Interval 400 (2 seeds): DEFECT in 2/2, the body fails after the 2nd–3rd replacement and never recovers. **Neither slot completeness nor chirality persists** under complete turnover at these rates; the identity of the body is not carried by the cell population. ESTABLISHED.

**(c) Extrusion** (each of the 24 cells pulled 8 units outward from the centroid, 300 time units). The cell rejoins the body (nearest-neighbour distance < 1.6) in **23/24** runs (the exception: one tail cell, 6.9 away). The body ends **L in 13/24 and DEFECT in 11/24** — the cell returns but often with a swap/duplicate of slots (22 slots filled once: a pair of cells exchanged slots). By type: type 1 L 4 / DEFECT 4; type 2 L 4 / DEFECT 0; type 3 L 3 / DEFECT 1; type 4 L 1 / DEFECT 7 (tail cells are the most fragile). ESTABLISHED.

**(d) Cut** (body halves separated by 7 units, along the body y axis (+y vs −y half) and the x axis (head vs tail half); 400 units). Fragments: y cut: 11 + 13 cells, x cut: 12 + 12; after 400 units the cells are in contact again (minimum inter-fragment distance 0.3 / 0.0, i.e. the fragments came back together or interpenetrated) and the whole body is DEFECT (4–6 components, 14–15 vacant slots, duplicates). **No fragment regenerates the missing half and the pieces do not re-fuse into one body.** ESTABLISHED (one body per cut).

**(e) Fusion** (two adult bodies placed at offset 9 (touching), 8, or (8,3), 400 units). L+L and L+R, all three placements (6 runs): the bodies **interpenetrate and both end defective** (6–10 components, 13–17 vacant slots among the 48 cells; the single exception, L+R at offset 9, kept body A's 24 slots filled once but in 4 components); no run produced one complete body or two intact bodies. ESTABLISHED.

## Summary
Cell replacement is repaired in 9/24 cases, extrusion in 13/24 (the cell almost always returns), serial replacement, cut and fusion destroy the body. These are the behaviours of a relational model whose per-cell identity beliefs are saturated (relaxation ~2 600 time units): once a neighbourhood is disturbed, slot assignments are rearranged rather than repaired. The identity-stress dataset (DATASETS.md) is built from exactly these events.
