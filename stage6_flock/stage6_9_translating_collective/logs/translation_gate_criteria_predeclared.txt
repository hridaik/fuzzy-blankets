PREDECLARED TRANSLATION FEASIBILITY CRITERIA -- Stage 6.9
Written BEFORE code/run_translation_gate.py was run, and before any Stage 6.9
number existed. These operationalize task brief section 33 verbatim and are
applied unchanged thereafter. No steering experiment may run until this gate is
evaluated.

An EPISODE is one uncontrolled realization. Its single longest tracked
collective is evaluated (chosen by tracked duration alone -- never by
displacement, turnover, or any identity score).

  brief: "a detected collective persists for many steps"
    T1  tracked duration                       >= 40 steps

  brief: "it translates by multiple interaction radii"
    T2  net centroid displacement              >= 3 * R      (R = 1.6)

  brief: "membership genuinely changes"
    T3  final material retention R_M           <= 0.70       (>=30% replaced)

  brief: "co-moving functional similarity remains high"
    T4  mean R_F over the tracked window       >= 0.70

  brief: "the group remains nontrivial and spatially coherent"
    T5  size within [0.03N, 0.55N] AND <= 2 spatial components,
        on >= 90% of tracked steps

An episode PASSES iff all five hold. The GATE passes iff the pass rate is
>= 0.20 over the screened seeds, i.e. the phenomenon is repeatable rather than
a single lucky realization.

OUTCOMES THAT ARE NOT A PASS, and how each is to be reported:
  * episodes translate but keep essentially their members (T3 fails, R_M ~ 1):
    report plainly as MOVING MATERIAL IDENTITY; do NOT claim the
    constituent-replacement result.
  * no persistent translating collective emerges at all: STOP. Do not force a
    prechosen moving cluster and call it emergence.
  * T4 fails while T1-T3 hold: the collective is being destroyed and replaced
    rather than transported; report as such.

The parameters (N=400, L=24, R=1.6, v=0.5, beta=1.0, rho=15, omega=3) are
fixed here. beta/rho/omega are the frozen Stage 6-6.8 values. R and v come from
the scratch feasibility prototype recorded in PLAN.md, chosen so that the
expected local degree is on the scale of the Moore model's 8 (4.6 at uniform
density, ~10.7 realized once the flock clusters) -- NOT chosen by any identity
or control outcome, none of which existed when they were fixed.

ADDENDUM 1 (Stage 6.9), written after the gate passed and before boundary
inference was run on real episodes.

GATE OUTCOME: pass rate 0.40 (16/40 episodes), against a 0.20 threshold.
Per-criterion rates: T1 persistence 1.00, T2 translation 0.90, T3 membership
change 0.50, T4 co-moving similarity 1.00, T5 coherence 0.95. **T3 is the
binding criterion**: in half the episodes the same birds simply travel
together (moving material identity), and those episodes are reported as such,
not as constituent replacement.

PROBE BUDGET for the moving model, fixed here by a COMPUTE CEILING (~4 minutes
per timepoint), not by any recovery score:

    60 rollouts per arm, 3 independent CRN repeats per (source, alternative
    heading), probe candidates within 1.1*R of the collective

The moving flock is far denser than the lattice: a clustered 100-bird group has
~113 birds inside a 2.6 radius and its true interface |B_t^D| is ~47 of ~63
nearby exterior birds, against ~20 of ~50 at Stage 6.8. Whatever recall this
budget buys is reported as-is; the budget is not raised or lowered afterwards
to improve it.

ADDENDUM 2 (Stage 6.9), written after the first boundary-inference timepoint.

The marginal-KL probe estimator carried over from Stage 6.8 returns ALL-ZERO
samples on the moving flock, so its bootstrap lower bound is 0 for nearly every
source and Bhat^causal comes back with one member out of a true interface of
~47. The cause is visible in the probe's own output, with no reference to any
oracle quantity: a highly polarized collective has a near-saturated policy
posterior, so intervening on one of ~10 live in-neighbours shifts an interior
bird's next-heading MARGINAL by far less than the sampling noise of two
independent 60-rollout histograms, and every sampled KL rounds to the smoothing
floor.

The estimator is therefore changed to the statistic the common random numbers
actually afford -- the PAIRED DISCREPANCY RATE

    Chat^do_{j->i} = (1/n) sum_k 1[ z_i^{do}(k) != z_i^{nat}(k) ]

computed from exactly the same rollouts, exactly the same budget, and exactly
the same decision rule (bootstrap CI lower bound > 0). It is zero for every
unaffected bird by construction and strictly positive for an affected one. The
marginal KL is still computed and stored alongside as `D_do_joint_kl`, so the
two can be compared.

This was changed for statistical power, on evidence internal to the probe, NOT
because of any recall figure against B_t^D -- no oracle comparison had been run
when it was made. Stage 6.8's L2/L3 results are NOT re-run or restated: they
keep the marginal-KL estimator they were produced with.

ADDENDUM 3 (Stage 6.9) -- CORRECTION to ADDENDUM 2.

ADDENDUM 2 asserted that the paired discrepancy rate would have usable power
where the marginal-KL estimator did not. **That was wrong, and the measurement
says so.** On the same candidate, the same budget and the same decision rule:

    marginal KL          : |Bhat^causal| = 1 of |B_t^D| = 47   (precision 1.00)
    paired discrepancy   : |Bhat^causal| = 1 of |B_t^D| = 47   (precision 1.00)

The two estimators agree exactly. The problem is therefore NOT that the KL
throws away the pairing; it is that the effect itself is below the resolution
of any one-step estimator at this budget. A polarized collective's policy
posterior is near-deterministic, so intervening on one of ~10 live in-neighbours
almost never flips an interior bird's realized next heading within 60 rollouts,
and the paired difference is genuinely zero in most rollouts rather than merely
buried in histogram noise. Detecting it would need on the order of 1/p ~ 10^3
rollouts per arm, i.e. ~3.4M simulator steps per timepoint.

The paired estimator is KEPT (it is the more natural statistic given common
random numbers, it is no worse, and it stores the KL alongside for comparison),
but the claim in ADDENDUM 2 that it fixes the power problem is retracted.

The correct reading is a SCIENTIFIC RESULT, not a defect to be engineered
around: at this model's internal order, the causal interface of a translating
collective is IDENTIFIABLE (the exact propagator resolves it) but effectively
UNESTIMABLE by one-step finite probing at any feasible budget. That is the
Stage 6.8 identifiability-vs-estimation gap taken to its limit, and it is
reported as such in RESULTS_6_9.md. No estimator, budget or threshold is
adjusted afterwards to make the number look better.

ADDENDUM 4 (Stage 6.9) -- MODEL RE-SPECIFICATION. Written after diagnosing the
degenerate causal interface and BEFORE the replacement (R, v) was chosen.

WHAT WENT WRONG. PROTOCOL_6_9 section 2 records R = 1.6 as giving "expected
degree 4.6 at uniform density, ~10.7 realized", against the brief's requirement
that local degree stay "on roughly the same scale as the original Moore model"
(8 geometric, ~5 live after FOV). Both figures were wrong as a description of
the steady state: measured directly in the clustered configuration the flock
actually occupies, the mean live IN-DEGREE is 21.2 -- about 3x the Moore scale
and 4x the FOV-live scale.

WHAT THAT BROKE. With ~21 contributing neighbours the expected-free-energy gap
between an interior bird's best and second-best action is ~93, so
`policy_posterior` saturates: 77% of interior birds have max(ut) == 1.0 to
double precision. A saturated decision cannot be moved by one neighbour, so the
one-step causal effect of intervening on almost any exterior source is
numerically zero. Measured on seed 0, t=60, |I| = 100, |B_t^D| = 47:

    Bhat^causal (finite probe, 60x3)   :  1 of 47
    B^causal,exact (exact propagator)  :  1 of 47      <-- the same
    effects above 1e-9                 :  1 of 63 candidates

So the near-empty interface was NOT an estimation failure and NOT an
identifiability-vs-estimation gap -- it was a direct consequence of a
mis-specified interaction radius. Reporting it as a scientific finding would
have been reporting an artefact of my own parameter choice.

THE FIX, and the rule for choosing it, declared here before the numbers are
seen. Candidate settings pair a smaller R with v scaled to preserve the
neighbour-crossing time v/R = 0.31:

    (R, v) in { (0.9, 0.28), (1.1, 0.34), (1.3, 0.40) }

    RULE: choose the pair whose MEAN REALIZED LIVE IN-DEGREE, measured in the
    clustered steady state of uncontrolled runs, is closest to the Moore
    model's 8. This uses uncontrolled phenomenology only -- no identity score,
    no interface recovery, no control outcome.

If the translation feasibility gate then FAILS at the chosen setting, that is
the pilot's outcome and it is reported as such. R is NOT moved back up to
recover a passing gate: a gate that passes only at a degree 3x the Moore scale
is a gate passed by a model that violates the stated specification.

Everything downstream of the gate -- the gate itself, boundary inference, the
guidance experiment -- is re-run at the corrected setting. The Stage 6.9
results produced at R = 1.6 are superseded, not reported.
