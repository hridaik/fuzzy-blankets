## Summary of established facts (prior passes, cited)

- Qualification t0=35, target_heading=2, 21-member interior.
- In the ACTUAL recorded trajectory, the target genuinely fragments at
  t=47 into two material daughters (18 and 16 members); the larger
  (followed) daughter persists to episode end with a modest alignment
  rise (0.05→0.35); the smaller daughter loses detectable coherence
  within 3 steps and is never re-acquired; neither daughter is the
  population that reaches v1's reported 0.86 — that population is a
  third, wholly unrelated, zero-overlap population.
- Classification: **likely physical split**, not confirmed (no validated
  threshold exists to elevate it), not mere candidate/detector
  fragmentation either (persistent separation, no re-fusion).

## New in this pass (Task F2)

Repeated-stochastic-replicate schedule-effect comparison (20–25
replicates, CRN-paired, `seed_503_disruption_analysis.md`):
- **Intervention effect on material-target alignment: demonstrated**
  (paired mean +0.221 vs. no forcing, 90% CI [0.099, 0.344]).
- **Actuator selectivity: not demonstrated** (historical vs. matched
  random paired mean −0.037, CI crosses zero).
- **Organizational disruption: not demonstrated as intervention-specific**
  — split events occur in >50% of replicates under ALL THREE conditions
  including no forcing; the historical-vs-no-forcing paired difference in
  split occurrence is centered near zero.

## What this means, read together

The actual recorded episode's large reported turn (0.86) does not belong
to the qualification-time material target — that remains an established,
unchanged fact from prior passes. Separately, this pass's repeated-replicate
evidence shows that (a) sustained forcing DOES tend to raise the material
target's own alignment relative to leaving it unforced, and (b) this
flock/regime fragments often regardless of forcing, so the ACTUAL
episode's t=47 split is not distinguishable, on this evidence, from
ordinary regime-level fragmentation risk. **Neither finding rescues the
historical "seed 503 = successful control" claim**: even setting the
identity/split question aside entirely, the schedule-effect evidence does
not show the specific historical actuator choice outperforming random
alternatives, so "learned actuator selection" is not supported here
either.
