================================================================
STAGE 6.11 -- INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION OF THE SECTION M AUDIT
Written while implementing moving_flock_611.py, before any 6.11 experiment.
================================================================
`logs/moving_model_audit.txt` was read as given and its three findings were
re-derived from the frozen code rather than accepted. This file records the
re-derivation, two refinements, and one quantitative confirmation that is
stronger than the argument the audit itself gives. There is no finding I
disagree with.

----------------------------------------------------------------
1. ALIGNMENT -- CONFIRMED, with a routing note
----------------------------------------------------------------
The audit cites flock_sim/model.py base_R():
        R = fc * (UV4 @ UV4.T); np.fill_diagonal(R, vm)      # vm=4, fc=1
Confirmed. Note for precision: R is not itself a reward over actions. It is
passed through neighbor_likelihood() = softmax_cols(R, beta) to become
A[o, s] = P(observe partner heading o | my heading s), which the diagonal makes
peaked at o = s. Alignment reaches the decision through the Risk term, which
compares A @ Bu[:, a] against a preference C peaked at the partner's LAST
OBSERVED heading. The effect is as the audit describes -- match your partners'
headings -- but "the diagonal rewards adopting the same heading" is a shorthand
for a likelihood, not a payoff.

----------------------------------------------------------------
2. SEPARATION -- CONFIRMED, and it is CONDITIONAL, not positional
----------------------------------------------------------------
The audit cites SLOT_OVERRIDES setting R[o, s] = -ca. Confirmed; e.g. slot 0
(partner directly above) overrides (o=1, s=0) = (partner observed heading DOWN,
my heading UP), i.e. the head-on pair.

REFINEMENT the audit does not state: the penalty is conditional on the
PARTNER'S HEADING as well as on its octant. A partner directly above me that is
heading up costs me nothing for heading up; only one that is heading down (into
me) does. So this is a collision-course term, not an occupancy repulsion. That
matters for ingredient 3, because it means the octant on its own -- the only
positional information in the model -- carries no unconditional preference.

----------------------------------------------------------------
3. POSITIONAL COHESION -- CONFIRMED ABSENT, exactly
----------------------------------------------------------------
The audit argues from construction (base_R is built from an inner product of
HEADING vectors; compute_G weights every live partner identically). Both checks
reproduce. A sharper numerical statement is available and was measured:

For each Moore slot, take the complete per-edge contribution the simulator adds
to a bird's G, G_table[slot, a] + Risk_table[slot, a, h], and average it over
the partner's heading h. The result, to printed precision:

    slot        up        down      left      right
    top        -4.778    -4.778    -4.778    -4.778
    down       -4.778    -4.778    -4.778    -4.778
    left       -4.778    -4.778    -4.778    -4.778
    right      -4.778    -4.778    -4.778    -4.778
    topleft    -4.778    -4.778    -4.778    -4.778
    downright  -4.778    -4.778    -4.778    -4.778
    downleft   -4.778    -4.778    -4.778    -4.778
    topright   -4.778    -4.778    -4.778    -4.778

Identical in every cell. Averaged over what the partner is doing, knowing WHERE
the partner is changes a bird's preference over its four headings by exactly
nothing. That is the audit's finding, stated as an equality rather than as an
argument, and it is the gap `cohesion=fc_pos` fills.

REFINEMENT: a weak positional dependence does exist before that average is
taken. The ambiguity table G_table[slot, a] varies with slot -- for a partner
at topleft it favours 'up' and 'left' equally, i.e. TOWARD the partner, by
0.139 in expected-free-energy units (0.043 for the four axis-aligned slots).
So there is a faint centering pressure in the model already. It is ~40x smaller
than the risk term's ~5.7 scale it sits beside, and, as the table above shows,
the Risk term cancels it exactly on average over the partner's heading. It does
not change the audit's conclusion, but "no term depending on neighbours'
positions" is true of the NET contribution, not of every table.

----------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE AUDIT'S HYPOTHESIS ABOUT STAGE 6.9 -- NOT VERIFIED HERE
----------------------------------------------------------------
The audit proposes that with alignment as the only binding force, cohesion must
be bought with interaction range, and range buys saturation. That is a
hypothesis and is treated as one. `run_model_validation.py` V4 tests its first
half (does positional cohesion hold a group together at lower realized degree)
and V3 tests the mechanism the second half assumes. Results, including where
they do not support the hypothesis, are in logs/model_validation_611.txt.
