PREDECLARED MESOSCOPIC CRITERIA -- Stage 6.8
Written AFTER data/phase_scan.json (primary beta grid + s-grid at beta=1.0) was
inspected and found to contain NO intermediate regime, and BEFORE the secondary
(beta, s) cross scan was run. These four criteria operationalize the task
brief's section-2 wording verbatim and are applied unchanged thereafter.

  brief: "coherent groups form"
    M1  mean_largest_component            >= 10 birds

  brief: "they persist"
    M2  mean_component_lifetime           >= 5 steps

  brief: "the entire lattice is not almost always one aligned component"
    M3a frac_time_largest_ge_90           <= 0.20
    M3b mean_largest_component            <= 70 birds

  brief: "substantial stochastic variation remains"
    M4a mean_membership_turnover          >= 0.05
    M4b mean_heading_entropy              >= 0.40 nats   (of log 4 = 1.386 max)

A grid cell is MESOSCOPIC iff all six hold. If more than one cell qualifies,
the two most distinct qualifying cells are retained (task brief: "If two
regimes look equally reasonable, retain both instead of selecting post hoc").
Ties are broken by distance from the primary grid's frozen L0 point
(beta=1.0, s=1.0) -- NOT by any downstream inference or control metric, which
do not exist yet and are not importable from run_phase_scan.py.

ADDENDUM 3, written after the L2 refinement, before any cell was selected.

The L2 refinement (data/phase_scan_fov_refine.json) again yields no qualifying
cell: across ~70 scanned cells at L1 and L2 the persistence criterion (M2) and
the non-globality criteria (M3a/M3b) are anti-correlated everywhere.

Inspecting the PER-SEED values behind the seed-averages shows why, and shows
that the seed-average was the wrong summary: the distribution is strongly
BIMODAL. At beta=1.0, s=2.0 (L2) the per-seed frac_time_largest_ge_90 values
are {1.0, .32, .12, .31, 0, 0, .44, 0, .68, 0, .36, .22, 0, .83, .19, .59,
1.0, 0, 0, .74} -- some realizations lock into a single global component
(lifetime 81 = the whole measured window), others never do (largest component
12-52 birds, lifetime 5-26). The across-seed SD of glob (0.34-0.40) is as
large as its mean. NO SINGLE REALIZATION resembles the seed-average, so a
seed-averaged criterion describes nothing that actually happens.

CHANGE OF EVALUATION LEVEL (not of thresholds). The six criteria M1, M2, M3a,
M3b, M4a, M4b are applied UNCHANGED, at the level they were always meant to
describe -- one uncontrolled realization. A seed is a "mesoscopic episode" iff
its own trajectory satisfies all six. A cell's score is the FRACTION of its
seeds that are mesoscopic episodes. Selection rule, declared here:

    choose the cell with the highest mesoscopic-episode fraction;
    ties broken by distance from the frozen L0 point (beta=1.0, s=1.0);
    if two cells remain equally reasonable, BOTH are retained and carried
    through the rest of the stage (task brief section 2).

Downstream inference and control are still not computable at this point and
are not importable from either scan script. No threshold has been moved.

ADDENDUM 4, written after the episode-level evaluation, before the size scan.

Episode-level result: at L2, ZERO of 20 seeds qualifies in ANY scanned cell.
The failures are informative rather than marginal:

  * beta=0.25 (all s)  : disordered churn, lifetime ~1  -> fails M2.
  * many beta>=0.5 seeds: the lattice FREEZES into a static two-heading,
    zero-polarization configuration (heading entropy exactly ln 2 = 0.693,
    polarization 0.006-0.04, membership turnover 0.004-0.009, component
    lifetime 26-80). These are absorbing states, not flocks, and M4a
    ("substantial stochastic variation remains") correctly rejects them.
  * the remaining seeds lock into a single global component.

At L1 the best cell (beta=1.25, s=0.5) qualifies on 6/20 seeds, and those are
FROZEN two-domain configurations (lifetime 62 with almost no turnover), i.e.
the same absorbing-state artefact one criterion short of being caught.

Before concluding "no mesoscopic regime exists in this model family", the
obvious confound must be excluded: FINITE SIZE. Every frozen Stage 6-6.7
result uses nn = 100 on a 10x10 free-boundary lattice with a Moore
neighbourhood, so each bird interacts with 8% of the entire flock and the
domain-wall length needed for two coexisting domains is ~10 sites. Mesoscopic
coexistence may simply not fit. This is a standard finite-size check, not a
model modification: the dynamics, the FOV rule and all six criteria are
untouched; only the lattice side length L changes.

SIZE SCAN (declared here, before it is run):
    nn in {100 (10x10), 225 (15x15), 400 (20x20), 900 (30x30)}
    beta in {0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5}, s = 1.0, at L2
    same 20 seeds, same nt, same burn-in, same six criteria, episode level.

If the mesoscopic-episode fraction stays at 0 as nn grows, the conclusion is
that this model family has no mesoscopic regime and the Stage 6.9 gate fails
on criterion 1. If it rises with nn, the smallest nn at which it is
substantial is adopted, and the deviation from the frozen nn=100 convention is
reported prominently as a scope change (Stage 6-6.7 results remain untouched
and are NOT re-run at the new size).

ADDENDUM 5, written after data/phase_scan_size.json.

SIZE SCAN RESULT: finite size was a real confound. The mesoscopic-episode
fraction is 0.00 at every beta for nn=100, rises to 0.10 (nn=225, beta=0.75)
and 0.15 (nn=400, beta=1.0), and falls back at nn=900. The largest coherent
component shrinks from 0.74N (nn=100) to 0.58N (nn=900) and the component
count rises from ~3.5 to ~7, i.e. coexisting domains DO appear once the
lattice is large enough to hold them -- but component lifetime falls with N
(3.2 at nn=400, 2.3 at nn=900), so the persistence criterion M2 becomes the
binding one at large N just as non-globality was at small N.

Since nn is now an axis, one further declared scan at the best size, criteria
unchanged, same selection rule (highest mesoscopic-episode fraction, ties by
distance from the frozen L0 point beta=1.0, s=1.0):

    nn = 400, beta in {0.75, 1.0, 1.25, 1.5}, s in {1.0, 1.5, 2.0}

nn is the ONLY deviation from the frozen Stage 6-6.7 convention that this
stage will make, it is forced by an uncontrolled-phenomenology finding, and it
is reported as a scope change in PHASE_MAP.md and RESULTS_6_8.md. No frozen
Stage 6-6.7 result is re-run or restated at the new size.

ADDENDUM 6, written after the first control run, before the arms were compared.

At the Stage-6 actuator budget (K_act = 10, horizon 16) the control task is
INFEASIBLE at this stage's scale, for a reason that has nothing to do with
which interface is actuated: Stage 6 steered a 20-bird interior on a 100-bird
lattice, whereas Stage 6.8's blind detector proposes collectives of 95-145
birds on a 400-bird lattice. The first run's frozen-causal arm reached
final_target_fraction = 0.000 with a mean of 4 actuators on a 119-bird
interior. Comparing five arms that all score zero would measure nothing.

FEASIBILITY CALIBRATION (declared here, before it is run). The horizon and the
actuator budget are set by asking ONLY whether the task is achievable at all,
using ONLY the adaptive_oracle arm -- the upper bound, which knows the true
interface -- on DEVELOPMENT seeds. The other four arms are not run, not
computed, and not compared during this step; their relative ordering therefore
cannot influence the choice.

    grid:  K_act in {10, 20, 30},  horizon in {16, 30}
    rule:  take the SMALLEST (K_act, horizon) at which the oracle arm's mean
           final target fraction on development seeds exceeds 0.5; if none
           does, report the task as infeasible at this scale and say so.

q_support is raised from 0.5 to 0.9 of the REACHABLE interior at the same time
and for the same reason: at 0.5 the multicover stopped after 4 actuators
having satisfied its own rule, so the budget was never the binding constraint
and the arms were never actually differentiated.

FEASIBILITY RESULT: the task is INFEASIBLE at this stage's scale. The
adaptive_oracle arm -- which is handed the true causal interface and the exact
influence matrix, i.e. the upper bound on any inferred method -- reaches a mean

This is a SCALE result, not an interface-inference failure. Stage 6 steered a
20-bird interior with a 12-bird boundary on a 100-bird lattice (ratio 0.6);
Stage 6.8's blind detector proposes collectives of 95-145 birds on a 400-bird
lattice, where an actuator set of 10-30 is 0.1-0.25 of the interface and the
interior's own consensus is 5-10 hops deep. Sparse boundary steering of a
20-bird collective does not transfer to a 120-bird one.

DECLARED FALLBACK, so that the five-arm comparison still answers its own
question ("does an adaptively re-inferred causal interface beat a frozen one?")
even though the absolute task is out of reach: run all arms at the grid's
best oracle setting (K_act = 20, horizon = 30), and report

    (a) the absolute levels, labelled as BELOW the feasibility threshold, and
    (b) the ORDERING of the arms, which is what the section-21 comparison is

One arm is added at the same time and for the same reason: `no_control`

ADDENDUM 8, written before the arm comparison was run to completion.

The control loop re-probes ONLINE at every re-inference step, which at the
offline pipeline's budget (50 rollouts x 6 repeats x ~45 sources, every 4
steps) costs ~650,000 simulator rollouts per adaptive episode. The control
loop is therefore given its own, smaller, separately declared probe budget:

    25 rollouts x 2 repeats, re-inferring every 6 steps

fixed here, applied identically to every arm that probes, and never varied
between arms. It is a HANDICAP on the two inferred-causal arms relative to
`adaptive_oracle` (which pays no estimation cost at all), so it cannot flatter
the method under test.

ADDENDUM 9, written after inspecting the first arms, before the comparison was
run to completion or interpreted.

Two evaluation flaws were found in the first arm run and fixed. Both were
found by looking at the arms' own bookkeeping (interior sizes and actuator
counts), not at which arm won.

1. SCORING SET. Each arm was being scored on the interior IT had re-detected
   at the final step. Those diverge sharply between arms -- 63, 84, 103 and 153
   birds on one development seed -- so an arm that merely let the collective
   drift into an already-aligned region scored well. Task success is now scored
   on I0, the collective detected at the control start, exactly as Stage 6
   scored it on its frozen interior. The current-interior score is still
   recorded as a secondary number.

2. BUDGET MATCHING. The multicover arms stop when their own coverage rule is
   satisfied, typically at ~10 actuators of a 20 cap, while `random_matched`
   always spent the full 20 -- i.e. the "matched-budget" control had twice the
   intervention budget of the method it was controlling for. `adaptive_causal`
   now runs first and its realized per-step actuator schedule is imposed on
   `random_matched` and `predictive`.

Both changes tighten the comparison against the method under test.
