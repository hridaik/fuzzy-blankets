================================================================
STAGE 6.10 PART H -- OPERATING-TASK SELECTION RULE
Written 2026-09-09, with 3 of 6 controllability cells observed
(frac=0.25/T=12, frac=0.25/T=24, frac=0.5/T=12) and 3 not yet run.
================================================================

PLAN.md requires a frozen operating task where the full-model control benchmark
"succeeds reliably but not trivially", and forbids running controller
comparison on episodes where full-information control cannot steer the
collective. This file operationalizes both before the remaining cells exist.

WHAT HAS ALREADY BEEN SEEN (and why it forces a per-episode rule):

  frac=0.25 T=12   meanH=0.450  >=0.60: 0.33   <=0.10: 0.33
  frac=0.25 T=24   meanH=0.492  >=0.60: 0.42   <=0.10: 0.25
  frac=0.5  T=12   meanH=0.474  >=0.60: 0.42   <=0.10: 0.42

The outcome is BIMODAL and barely responds to resources: doubling the horizon
moved the mean 0.450 -> 0.492, doubling the actuator fraction moved it
0.450 -> 0.474, and the fraction of completely-stuck episodes did not fall.
Whether an episode is steerable therefore looks like a property of the episode,
not of the control budget. A per-cell threshold alone would either discard the
whole regime or average steerable and unsteerable episodes together.

RULE 1 -- FROZEN CELL.
  Among cells with frac_above_hi >= 0.50, take the one with the SMALLEST
  resource cost, ordered by actuator fraction first, then horizon.
  Smallest-sufficient, not largest-success: selecting the most generous cell
  would make the task trivial and would flatter every arm equally.
  If NO cell reaches 0.50, take the cell with the highest frac_above_hi and
  record explicitly that reliability was not established at the cell level.

RULE 2 -- PRIMARY EPISODES (the per-episode gate).
  Within the frozen cell, an episode is a PROSPECTIVE PRIMARY EPISODE iff the
  full-model benchmark reached H*(I_t,t) >= 0.60 on it. Part I runs only on
  these. This is the direct implementation of "do not proceed with controller
  comparison on episodes where full-information control itself cannot steer the
  collective."

RULE 3 -- SCOPE OF CLAIMS.
  The number of excluded episodes is reported alongside every Part I result,
  and all Part I conclusions are scoped to the steerable stratum. No statement
  of the form "the method steers collectives at this regime" is made; the
  admissible form is "within the stratum where full-model control succeeds, the
  method does/does not match it".

RULE 4 -- WHAT MAY NOT HAPPEN.
  * The cell is not re-chosen after seeing any inference arm's performance.
  * The primary-episode set is defined by the BENCHMARK only, never by any
    inference arm, and is fixed before Part I runs.
  * Episodes are not added back after a failure, and the H threshold (0.60) is
    not lowered. It is already frozen in PROTOCOL_6_10.md as the conjunctive
    success threshold.
  * If the steerable stratum is too small to support a comparison, that is
    reported as the result. It is not fixed by enlarging the budget until
    everything becomes steerable.

ADDENDUM 1 -- 2026-09-09: the first controllability scan is SUPERSEDED
================================================================
While the scan was running (4 of 6 cells complete) a defect was found in the
benchmark's objective, not in the scan:

  `reference_truth.rollout_alignment` applied the intervention only at the FIRST
  rollout step (`if s == 0`), i.e. it evaluated "force the actuators now, then
  release, and look tau steps later". Execution does the opposite: `sim.step` is
  called with `forced_actions` on EVERY step between re-plans, so the actuators
  are held for the whole horizon.

The full-model benchmark was therefore planning a weaker intervention than the
one it actually carried out. The direction of the error matters for this
particular use: it makes the benchmark look LESS capable than it is, and Part H
uses the benchmark to decide which episodes are unsteerable. An
under-powered benchmark would have excluded steerable episodes and understated
how controllable the regime is.

FIX: `hold` parameter on `rollout_alignment` / `_expected_alignment_after`.
  * default hold = 1, so task authority A_j^{h*,tau} remains the one-shot
    quantity it is defined to be -- Part B's numbers are unchanged.
  * `full_model_benchmark.optimize` defaults to hold = tau, matching execution.
  * asserted in tests/test_reference_truth.py::
      test_hold_semantics_are_distinct_and_authority_stays_one_shot
    which also had to move to the Stage 6.10 regime: at the Stage 6.8 operating
    point both semantics return exactly 0.0, because nothing there is steerable
    at all.

The partial scan is retained as `data/controllability__SUPERSEDED_hold1.json`
and is not used for any claim. The scan is being re-run with the corrected
objective. The selection rules above are NOT modified: they were written before
either scan's outcome was known, and they apply unchanged to the new one.

ADDENDUM 1a -- what the correction actually changed, empirically.
The first corrected cell (frac=0.25, T=12) gives meanH = 0.410, against 0.450
under the superseded objective; frac_above_hi is 0.33 in both. With sd = 0.40
and n = 12 (se ~ 0.116) that difference is well inside noise, and it is in the
opposite direction from the one predicted when the fix was made.

Recorded explicitly so the re-run is not later read as an improvement: the fix
is justified because planning must model execution, NOT because it raised any
number. No measurable change in benchmark performance has been demonstrated.

ADDENDUM 2 -- 2026-09-09: second controllability scan SUPERSEDED
================================================================
The corrected (hold=tau) scan selected frac=0.5, T=24 and a 7-episode primary
stratum. Running Part I on that stratum exposed an inconsistency in the GATE,
not in Part I:

  run_controllability.run_benchmark_episode started its tracker with I_t = None,
  so `detect` seeded the lineage with the LARGEST detected candidate. Part I
  (closed_loop.run_arm, I_init=I0) steers the QUALIFYING start -- the
  most-clump-like moderate-size candidate. The gate and the comparison were
  therefore about different collectives.

Symptom that revealed it: all 7 primary episodes were admitted because the
benchmark reached H >= 0.60 on them, yet in Part I the same benchmark met the
task on only 4 of 7 (taskOK = 0.57). Episodes had been admitted on the strength
of steering a collective Part I never touches.

FIX (run_controllability.py):
  * the gate's tracker is seeded with the qualifying start I0 and continued by
    maximum overlap, identical to closed_loop.run_arm;
  * the actuator budget is fixed from the interface size at t0 and held for the
    episode, also identical to Part I. A budget re-derived from each arm's own
    current interface would differ per arm and make "matched budget" undefined,
    so "actuator fraction of the current causal interface" is evaluated at the
    moment control begins.

`data/controllability__SUPERSEDED_largestcand.json` is retained and used for no
claim. Selection RULES 1-4 above are unchanged -- they predate both scans.

The Part I run on the superseded stratum is also discarded rather than reported:
its episode list was chosen by a gate that measured a different object.

ADDENDUM 3 -- 2026-09-09: third scan superseded; gate now DELEGATES
================================================================
Scan 3 fixed the tracker and the budget, and the frozen cell stayed
frac=0.5 / T=24. But Part I's benchmark arm STILL met the task on only 4 of the
7 episodes that the gate had admitted for reaching H >= 0.60.

Cause: the gate reimplemented the control loop instead of calling Part I's. The
two had drifted apart in three places at once --

  candidate pool   gate used B_struct;      Part I used B_do (exact-do over
                                            near_exterior candidates)
  rollout count    gate 32;                 Part I 48
  CRN seed         gate seed*977 + step;    Part I seed*1000 + step

so the gate's benchmark run was never the run Part I reproduced. Two separate
problems were tangled here and both are now addressed:

1. CONFIGURATION DRIFT. `run_benchmark_episode` now delegates to
   `closed_loop.run_arm("full_model_benchmark", ...)` with exactly the arguments
   Part I uses, including `budget_schedule=None` (Part I runs this arm first, so
   the schedule is None there too). The gate and the comparison are now the same
   code on the same episode with the same random streams, and cannot drift again
   without both moving together.

2. SELECTION ON A NOISY MEASUREMENT. Even with identical code, choosing the top
   episodes by one benchmark run and then RE-RUNNING with different randomness
   would regress toward the mean, so the benchmark would "fail" some episodes it
   was selected for. Delegation removes this too, because the gate's run and
   Part I's benchmark arm are now bit-for-bit the same run rather than two draws.

CONSEQUENCE THAT MUST BE STATED WHEREVER PART I IS REPORTED:
  the benchmark's task-success rate within the primary stratum is 1.0 BY
  CONSTRUCTION -- the stratum is defined as the episodes it succeeds on. That
  number is a selection artefact, not a measurement, and is never quoted as
  evidence that the benchmark is good. Its IDENTITY-VALIDITY rate is a real
  measurement, since validity plays no part in the selection.

Retained and unused: controllability__SUPERSEDED_pathdrift.json,
data/superseded_partI_v2/. Selection RULES 1-4 remain as first written.
