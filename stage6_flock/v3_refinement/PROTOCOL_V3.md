## What Part 1 actually found (not what was expected)

The task brief's headline hypothesis was "distributed coverage of the true
interaction shell matters more than individual actuator strength," operationalized
as `Gamma(A)` = fraction of the core with at least one actuated neighbor.
Fitting nested logistic models on 10,000 development-flock replicate-level
outcomes (`data/success_model_fits.json`) found:

| Model | features | in-sample McFadden R² | LOO-holdout logloss |
|---|---|---|---|
| `f_A` alone | `f_A` | 0.420 | 0.420 |
| `Gamma` alone | `Gamma` (= `frac_m_ge1`) | **0.379** | **0.435** |
| `frac_m_ge2` alone | `frac_m_ge2` | 0.576 | 0.294 |
| `mean_m` alone | `mean_m` | **0.610** | **0.274** |
| `Gamma + mean_m` | both | 0.610 | 0.281 |

**Plain `Gamma` is the *weakest* of these single predictors — slightly worse
than raw actuator fraction `f_A`.** The strongest single predictor is `mean_m`
(average number of actuated neighbors per core bird — a redundancy/depth
measure), with `frac_m_ge2` ("double coverage") close behind. This is the
opposite of what a naive reading of "coverage matters" would predict, and it
is reported exactly as found, per the task brief's own stopping rule ("if
success does not collapse onto interface coverage, report that... another
structural quantity matters more").

**Interpretation, not just curve-fitting**: `Gamma` saturates quickly as
`f_A` grows (shell members' neighbor sets overlap heavily on this Moore
lattice, so most of the core acquires *some* actuated neighbor well before
the shell is half-forced) and then stays pinned at ~1.0 while `p_success`
keeps climbing — `Gamma` loses all discriminative power exactly in the
regime where more actuators still help. What is still improving in that
regime is redundancy: how many independent, possibly-noisy "votes" toward
the target heading each core bird receives, given that the model's own
update rule (`compute_G`) pools weighted evidence across up to 8 neighbors,
so a single forced neighbor is one vote that ordinary noise in the other
7 can still overrule.

## Part 1D — the frozen minimal-sufficient-interface criterion

Reformulating the criterion from single-coverage (`Gamma(A) >= gamma`) to
**double-coverage** (`frac(I0 with m_i(A) >= 2) >= gamma`, i.e. `q=2`
multicover) and sweeping `gamma in {0.5,...,1.0}` via greedy multicover
(`code/selection_rules_v3.py:min_actuators_for_multicover`) on the 10 dev
flocks:

| q | gamma | mean k | mean f_A | dev-mean P(success) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (plain Gamma) | 0.5 | 2.9 | 0.182 | 0.110 |
| 1 | 1.0 | 15.8 | 0.929 | 0.990 |
| **2 (double coverage)** | **0.5** | **8.6** | **0.544** | **0.855** |
| 2 | 0.6 | 12.9 | 0.782 | 0.990 |
| 2 | 1.0 | 17.0 | 1.000 | 0.990 |

Under `q=1`, dev-mean `p_success` never crosses 0.8 until `gamma=1.0` — i.e.
plain single-coverage is not a useful sparsification target at all; you
need to touch essentially the whole core's neighborhood before it predicts
success. Under `q=2`, the pre-registered rule ("smallest `gamma` whose
dev-mean `p_success>=0.8`", identical in spirit to how V2 chose its 0.75
fraction) selects **`gamma=0.5`, q=2** — needing on average only **54% of
the shell** (vs. V2's blanket 75%) for comparable dev-flock success. This is
the frozen V3 controller: **greedily build a set that gives at least half of
the core two or more actuated neighbors.** `gamma=0.6` (mean `f_A=0.78`,
dev-mean success 0.990) is reported as a secondary reference point — closer
to V2's fraction and near-ceiling success — but is **not** substituted for
the frozen `gamma=0.5` choice, which follows the rule fixed in
`REFINEMENT_PLAN.md` before this sweep was run.

## Part 2 — frozen transition-aware integrity criterion

Baseline coherence-transition statistics (`code/analyze_baseline_transition.py`,
200 pooled replicate trajectories per condition, dev flocks):

| Condition | C_min never below | recovery-to-0.8 (dwell=3) achieved | p90 recovery time | max recovery time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| full shell (`f_A=1.0`) | 0.500 | 100.0% | 9.1 steps | 17 steps |
| chosen sparse (`q=2, gamma=0.5`) | 0.421 | 99.5% | 0.0 steps (bimodal) | 16 steps |

Frozen thresholds, chosen from these numbers (not from how good they make
any controller look): `c_recover = 0.8` (same numeric bar as the *original*
criterion — the change is *when* it is checked, not a weaker bar),
`dwell_steps = 3` (a momentary crossing does not count), `T_rec = T_u = 20`
(comfortably above the observed worst case of 17 steps, without being set
equal to that worst case). Full criterion, and the honest statement that the
**original** `C_{I0}(t)>=0.8`-at-every-timestep bar is retained unmodified
and reported alongside, never replaced: see `INTEGRITY_DEFINITION.md` and
`configs/protocol_v3.yaml`'s `integrity_v3` block.

## What would constitute a V3 failure, stated in advance

- If held-out flocks (Part 1E) need a systematically larger `f_A` at the
  frozen `gamma=0.5,q=2` criterion to reach comparable success, that is
  reported as a generalization gap, not an occasion to re-tune `gamma`
  inside this session.
- If staged actuation (Part 2D) does not reduce `C_min` or recovery time
  relative to Pulse, that is reported as "the coherence dip is intrinsic to
  this state transition," per the task brief's own stopping rule — not
  papered over with a more complex schedule invented after seeing the
  negative result.
- If predictive leakage (Part 3) is flat/zero at every `tau` for both `B^D`
  and `B^F`, that is investigated analytically before being reported as
  surprising.
- If `B^F` performs comparably to `B^D` at some horizon, that is reported
  plainly, not explained away.
